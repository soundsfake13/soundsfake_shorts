# Day 42 — Dolphins sleep with half their brain (23:00 slot, 2026-11-14)

Sources checked this run:
- Scientific American, "How do whales and dolphins sleep without drowning?" — https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/how-do-whales-and-dolphin/
  One hemisphere sleeps while the other stays awake at a low level; the eye opposite the sleeping half closes; breathing
  is voluntary, so the awake half handles surfacing to breathe; after about two hours the halves swap.
- Smithsonian Magazine, "Dolphins Sleep With Only Half Their Brain at a Time" — https://www.smithsonianmag.com/smart-news/dolphins-sleep-with-only-half-their-brain-at-a-time-81426439/
  Unihemispheric sleep; in a PLoS ONE study two dolphins stayed alert for 15 days (test ended at 15 days); thought to
  let them breathe at the surface while sleeping.
- Lyamin et al., review on cetacean sleep (PMC8742503) — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8742503
  Unihemispheric slow-wave sleep; the eye contralateral to the sleeping hemisphere is usually closed, the other open
  (95–98% of the time in a bottlenose dolphin and a beluga); the open eye has a sentinel role; at least one active
  hemisphere is associated with breathing.

Wording kept conservative: "about two hours", "in one study ... fifteen days", "the eye linked to the sleeping half".
