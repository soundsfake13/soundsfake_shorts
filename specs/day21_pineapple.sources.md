# Day 21 — A pineapple takes about two years to grow (sources checked 2026-11-17)

- **UF/IFAS Extension, "Pineapple Growing in the Florida Home Landscape" (MG055)** — https://ask.ifas.ufl.edu/publication/MG055
  Time from planting to fruit "ranges from 18 to 24 months" (up to 36 in subtropical climates); a plant produces a main
  fruit and later a second (ratoon) fruit from a sucker — one at a time; the fruit is a syncarp, "a fruit derived from the
  fusion of many individual flowers into one fruit."
- **Encyclopaedia Britannica, "Pineapple"** — https://www.britannica.com/plant/pineapple
  Commercial plants form the inflorescence ~15–20 months after planting and the fruit ripens 5–6 months later; the
  "originally separate light purple flowers" become fleshy and fuse to form the fruit; leaves grow as a rosette on a short stem.
- **Live Science, "How Do Pineapples Grow?"** — https://www.livescience.com/44499-how-do-pineapples-grow.html
  Pineapples don't grow on trees but out of the ground from a leafy plant; the fruit is the result of dozens of
  individual flowers fused into a single fruit.
- **Eden Project, "Pineapple"** — https://www.edenproject.com/visit/things-to-do/rainforest-biome/pineapple
  Fruit is composed of 100–200 fused berry-like fruitlets (supports "dozens" of flowers).

Claims used: ~18–24 months to first fruit (UF + Britannica), one pineapple per plant at a time (UF; Britannica: one
inflorescence per stalk), grows from the ground on a spiky rosette plant (Live Science + Britannica), dozens of tiny purple
flowers fuse into one fruit (Britannica + Live Science + UF + Eden).
