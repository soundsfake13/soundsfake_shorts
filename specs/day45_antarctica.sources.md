# Day 45 — Antarctica is the biggest desert on Earth (sources checked 2026-11-16)

- **Live Science — "What is the largest desert on Earth?"** (quotes polar scientist Jonathan Wille): a region with less than
  25 cm (9.8 in) of precipitation a year qualifies as a desert; "At 14.2 million square kilometers, Antarctica is the largest
  desert in the world"; Sahara ~9.2 million km², the largest hot desert. https://www.livescience.com/largest-desert-on-earth
- **Guinness World Records — Largest desert (cold)**: Antarctic Ice Sheet, ~14 million km²; the interior can get as little as
  50 mm (2 in) of precipitation a year (water equivalent). https://www.guinnessworldrecords.com/world-records/largest-desert-cold
- **Encyclopaedia Britannica — Antarctica: Climate**: about 2 in (50 mm) of precipitation per year over the polar plateau;
  "Antarctica must be considered one of the world's great deserts." https://www.britannica.com/place/Antarctica/Climate
- **Discovering Antarctica (British Antarctic Survey / RGS education site)**: the continent is a polar desert; less than 50 mm
  a year (water equivalent) across much of the interior, "hyper arid". https://discoveringantarctica.org.uk/?p=338
- **BBC Wildlife (Discover Wildlife) — largest desert in the world**: desert = less than 250 mm of precipitation a year;
  Antarctica ~14.2 million km². https://www.discoverwildlife.com/environment/largest-desert-in-the-world
- **Encyclopaedia Britannica — Sahara summary**: about 8.6 million km² (other sources ~9.2 million) → script says "about 9"
  (caption "~9"). https://www.britannica.com/summary/Sahara-desert-Africa
