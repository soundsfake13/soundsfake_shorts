# Day 16 — Hippos make their own sunscreen (sources checked 2026-11-12)

- Nature News, "Hippos beat the burn" (27 May 2004) — https://www.nature.com/news/2004/040524/full/news040524-7.html
  Red (hipposudoric acid) and orange (norhipposudoric acid) pigments; absorb UV, likely shield from UV rays; red pigment
  restricts growth of *Pseudomonas aeruginosa* and *Klebsiella pneumoniae*; "neither blood nor exactly sweat".
- Science News, "Red Sweat: Hippo skin oozes antibiotic sunscreen" (26 May 2004) — https://www.sciencenews.org/article/red-sweat-hippo-skin-oozes-antibiotic-sunscreen
  Secretion starts colorless, turns red-orange within minutes, then browns; pigments' absorption peaks in UV;
  red pigment inhibits two disease-causing bacteria; not technically sweat (Keith Eltringham).
- Journal of Experimental Biology, "Hippos lead the way in multipurpose sun screen" (2004) — https://cob.silverchair.com/jeb/article/207/19/v/14939/HIPPOS-LEAD-THE-WAY-IN-MULTIPURPOSE-SUN-SCREEN
  Clear when produced, red-brown within minutes; pigments absorb ~200–600 nm; red pigment an effective antibiotic.
- Original paper: Saikawa Y. et al., "Pigment chemistry: the red sweat of the hippopotamus", Nature 429, 363 (2004).
