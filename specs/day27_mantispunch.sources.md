# Day 27 — The mantis shrimp punch boils water (sources checked 2026-11-23)

| claim in the video | sources |
|---|---|
| Club swings at up to ~23 m/s (≈ 80 km/h / 50 mph) | UC Berkeley news release 2004 (Patek et al., *Nature*): "peak speeds of 23 meters per second"; Patek Lab, Duke: "12–23 m/s (in water!)"; National Geographic (Ed Yong, 2008): "about 50 mph"; Science News Explores (2013): "50 to 83 kilometers … per hour" |
| Accelerates at up to ~10,000 times gravity | National Geographic: "up to 10,000 times the force of gravity"; UT Marine Science Institute "A Simply Smashing Shrimp": "up to 10,000 times the acceleration of gravity". (Berkeley 2004 measured 6,300–8,000 g in its sample — hence "up to") |
| Water in front of the club boils into (cavitation) bubbles | National Geographic: "lowers the pressure of the water in front of it, causing it to boil"; Science News Explores: "they set the surrounding water boiling. This produces destructive bubbles that quickly collapse"; Patek Lab: "the water cavitates (vaporizes)" |
| Bubble collapse makes a bang, heat and a flash of light | Patek Lab, Duke: collapse "produces heat, light and sound"; AMNH Fast Facts: "implode with a sharp bang, extremely high heat, and a flash of light"; National Geographic: "small flashes of light upon impact" |
| Some have cracked aquarium glass | California Academy of Sciences: "can even crack aquarium glass"; AMNH: "strong enough to break … aquarium glass"; National Geographic: smashed "quarter-inch-thick glass"; UC Berkeley 2004 |

Dropped: the plan's "thousands of degrees" — no checked source gave a temperature figure, so the video only says "heat".
