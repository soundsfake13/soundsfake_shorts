# Day 29 — Tardigrades survived open space — sources checked this run (2026-11-25)

- ESA, "Tiny animals survive exposure to space" — ~3000 water bears on ESA's Foton-M3 mission, 12 days in September 2007; "first animals to survive exposure to space"; vacuum and cosmic radiation "were not a problem"; largest species just over 1 mm.
  https://www.esa.int/Our_Activities/Human_Spaceflight/Research/Tiny_animals_survive_exposure_to_space
- ScienceDaily / Cell Press release (9 Sept 2008, on Jönsson et al., Current Biology) — dried-up tardigrades on FOTON-M3, low Earth orbit ~270 km; most survived vacuum and cosmic rays; some survived solar UV >1000× Earth-surface levels; survivors reproduced; 0.1–1.5 mm.
  https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2008/09/080908135906.htm
- National Geographic (Ed Yong, 8 Sept 2008) — sent up in a dry dormant "tun" state; ten days in low Earth orbit; majority survived vacuum + cosmic radiation; most unshielded from UV died, a small number survived; laid viable eggs that hatched.
  https://nationalgeographic.com/science/article/tardigrades-become-first-animals-to-survive-vacuum-of-space
- Scientific American, "First Animal That Can Survive in Orbit" — Foton M3; vacuum alone survived very well; size 0.05–1.5 mm.
  https://scientificamerican.com/article/first-animal-that-can-survive-in-space
- Britannica, "Tardigrade" — mostly about 1 mm or less; body dries out into a lifeless-looking ball (tun).
  https://www.britannica.com/animal/tardigrade

Script choices: duration given as "over a week" (sources say 10 or 12 days); "most survived the vacuum", "a few even survived raw sunlight" (UV); "laid eggs that hatched" (NatGeo; ScienceDaily: survivors reproduced). No percentages used.
