# Day 47 — Astronauts get taller in space (23:00 slot, 2026-11-19)

Sources checked in this run:
- NASA Technical Reports Server, "Seated Height Growth in Microgravity" study (ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20110023150) — Apollo-Soyuz and Skylab data showed stature increases of up to about 3%; in 29 crewmembers stature grew up to 3% (seated height up to 6%); without gravity the spine's curve straightens and the column elongates; fluid shifts in the intervertebral disks may contribute.
- Space.com, "Strange But True: Astronauts Get Taller in Space" — https://www.space.com/19116-astronauts-taller-space-spines.html — NASA scientists: up to 3% taller; a 6-foot (1.8 m) person could gain up to about 2 inches (5 cm); height returns to normal after returning to Earth.
- Scientific American (same Space.com article, syndicated) — https://scientificamerican.com/article/astronauts-get-taller-in-space — same figures.
- Phys.org / Wolters Kluwer (UC San Diego study in *Spine*, NASA-funded) — https://phys.org/news/2016-10-space-affects-spine-astronauts.pdf — astronauts gain about two inches in height, thought to come from spinal "unloading" without gravity.
- Phys.org (AP), on a year-long ISS mission — https://phys.org/news/2016-03-sorebut-tallerastronaut-scott-kelly-adjusts.pdf — the spine temporarily expanded about 1.5 in; NASA's John Charles: comes from fluid build-up in the spinal discs and goes away soon after landing.

Wording choices: "up to 3% taller" (NASA NTRS + Space.com). "For a tall adult, about 5 cm" (Space.com: 6-ft person, ~2 in / 5 cm; UCSD ~2 in). Discs "can puff up with fluid" (NTRS "may contribute", NASA's Charles). "Back to normal" without a time claim (sources differ: quickly vs a few months).
