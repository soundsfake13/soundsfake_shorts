# Day 56 — Chickens are close living relatives of T. rex — sources (checked 2026-11-27)

- Harvard Gazette, "Molecular analysis confirms T. rex's evolutionary link to birds" (24 Apr 2008):
  https://news.harvard.edu/gazette/story/2008/04/molecular-analysis-confirms-t-rexs-evolutionary-link-to-birds/
  — 68-million-year-old T. rex protein; dinosaurs share common ancestry with chickens, ostriches and, to a lesser
  extent, alligators; Asara: T. rex "grouped with birds — ostrich and chicken — better than any other organism that we
  studied", and "better with birds than modern reptiles, such as alligators and green anole lizards". Published in Science.
- NPR, "T. Rex Protein Evidence Links Dinosaurs To Birds" (25 Apr 2008):
  https://www.npr.org/2008/04/25/89942780/t-rex-protein-evidence-links-dinosaurs-to-birds
  — 68-million-year-old T. rex leg bone; same Asara quotes on ostrich/chicken vs alligators and anole lizards.
- Scientific American, "Was T. Rex Really King of the Lizards—or Just a Big, Carnivorous Chicken?":
  https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/t-rex-protein-sequenced-similar-to-chicken/
  — collagen from a 68-million-year-old thighbone (Hell Creek Formation); closest similarity to chicken.
- Natural History Museum (London), "How dinosaurs evolved into birds":
  https://www.nhm.ac.uk/discover/how-dinosaurs-evolved-into-birds.html — "Birds ... are living dinosaurs".
- Australian Museum, "The living dinosaurs": https://australian.museum/The-living-dinosaurs
  — birds are theropod dinosaurs ("chickens ... they're all dinosaurs").

Wording choices: "closest living relatives" = birds (Harvard Gazette lede); protein comparison phrased as
"best matches ... chickens and ostriches" among the animals compared (lizards, alligators named in the studies);
"cousin" (shared ancestry, not descent from T. rex itself). claudevideo.org could not be reached this run.
