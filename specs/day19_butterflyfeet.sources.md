# Day 19 — Butterflies taste with their feet (sources checked 2026-11-15)

- Carleton University, Dept. of Biology — "Do butterflies really taste through their feet?": https://carleton.ca/biology/cu-faq/do-butterflies-really-taste-through-their-feet/
  (butterflies have contact chemoreceptors on their feet; a female taps leaves with her feet to release plant juices and checks for the right chemicals; she is testing whether the plant is right for her caterpillars, not food for herself)
- Washington State University, Ask Dr. Universe — "Do butterflies taste with their feet?": https://askdruniverse.wsu.edu/2026/07/16/butterflies-taste-feet/
  (taste structures on the feet; butterflies "drum" on plants with their feet; females have more foot taste receptors than males, to find host plants for their caterpillars)
- Ozaki et al., "A gustatory receptor involved in host plant recognition for oviposition of a swallowtail butterfly", Nature Communications 2 (2011), doi:10.1038/ncomms1548 — abstract via https://ideas.repec.org/a/nat/natcom/v2y2011i1d10.1038_ncomms1548.html
  (Papilio xuthus females detect host-plant chemicals with foreleg sensors while drumming on leaves; receptor PxutGr1)
- Phys.org, "Butterfly legs help pick the right spot to lay eggs" (Nov 2011): https://phys.org/news/2011-11-butterfly-legs-egg.amp
  (swallowtail females drum their forelegs on a leaf and lay eggs only when they detect specific chemicals)
