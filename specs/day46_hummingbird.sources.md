# Day 46 — Hummingbirds can fly backwards (23:00 slot, 2026-11-18)

Sources checked in this run:
- Smithsonian Gardens, "Hovering" — https://gardens.si.edu/exhibitions/traveling/habitat/hovering/ — "Unlike other birds, hummingbirds can fly in multiple directions, including backward and sideways"; "wings can beat up to 80 times per second".
- Encyclopaedia Britannica, "Hummingbird" — https://www.britannica.com/animal/hummingbird — wings attach to the body only at the shoulder joint, allowing flight forward, up, down, sideways and backward; ~80 beats/s in the tiny Calliphlox amethystina male, ~70 ruby-throated male, ~10 giant hummingbird.
- Encyclopaedia Britannica, "How Fast Do Hummingbirds Fly?" — https://www.britannica.com/science/How-Fast-Do-Hummingbirds-Fly — "up to 80 beats per second in some species".
- National Audubon Society, "Hummingbird Flight Is Like Nothing Else in the Bird World" — https://www.audubon.org/news/hummingbird-flight-nothing-else-bird-world — flies backwards and forwards; "the only bird that can truly hover"; 20–80 wingbeats a second.
- U.S. National Park Service (Cabrillo NM), "Hummingbirds: Aerial Acrobats" — https://www.nps.gov/cabr/blogs/hummingbirds-aerial-acrobats.htm — "the only type of birds that can truly hover"; wings move "in a figure eight"; can move in any direction including backwards.
- Sciencing, "Hummingbirds Are The Only Bird Species That Can Do This Trick" — https://www.sciencing.com/1864840/why-hummingbirds-only-bird-species-fly-backwards — only birds that fly backwards; ball-and-socket shoulder; figure-eight motion (secondary, unsourced article; used only as a supporting check).

Wording choices: "Unlike other birds, they can truly hover" (Audubon + NPS + Smithsonian). "Up to 80 times a second" with on-screen note "in the smallest species" (Britannica). "Swivels at the shoulder" (Britannica shoulder-only joint; Sciencing ball-and-socket). "Figure eight" (NPS + Sciencing). Did not claim a mechanism for how backward thrust is produced.
