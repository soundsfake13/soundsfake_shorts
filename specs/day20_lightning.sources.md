# Day 20 — Lightning is hotter than the Sun's surface (sources checked 2026-11-16)

- NOAA National Weather Service, "Lightning temperature" — lightning can heat the air it passes through to 50,000 °F
  (about 5 times hotter than the surface of the Sun). https://weather.gov/safety/lightning-temperature
- NOAA National Weather Service, "Lightning science overview" — same 50,000 °F / ~5× figure; lightning heats the air
  quickly, the air expands rapidly and creates the sound wave we hear as thunder. https://www.weather.gov/safety/lightning-science-overview
- Encyclopaedia Britannica, "Thunderstorm — Cloud-to-ground lightning" — peak channel temperatures on the order of
  30,000 °C (50,000 °F), roughly five times hotter than the Sun's surface. https://www.britannica.com/science/thunderstorm/Cloud-to-ground-lightning
- Encyclopaedia Britannica, "Thunder" — thunder is sound caused by a lightning discharge; lightning heats the air in its
  path and causes a large over-pressure in the channel. https://www.britannica.com/science/thunder
- BBC Science Focus, "5 times hotter than the Sun: why lightning is so powerful" — 30,000 °C, five times hotter than the
  Sun's surface. https://www.sciencefocus.com/planet-earth/why-lightning-is-so-powerful
- NASA Science, "Sun: Facts" — photosphere (visible surface) about 10,000 °F (5,500 °C). https://science.nasa.gov/sun/facts

Script wording: "about 5,500 °C", "can heat the air to about 30,000", "about five times hotter", "the air heats up so fast,
it bursts outward … that blast is the thunder you hear" (NWS: rapid expansion → sound wave; Britannica: over-pressure).
