# Day 22 — Some turtles breathe through their butts (sources checked 2026-11-18)

Claims used and where they were confirmed:
- Some turtles take oxygen from water through the cloaca (cloacal respiration); they pump water in and out of two
  sac-like organs (bursae) lined with papillae, where oxygen diffuses into the blood — Live Science; Wikipedia
  (Fitzroy River turtle: "two enlarged cloacal bursae ... lined with multibranching papillae"); Scientific American.
- The Fitzroy River turtle (Rheodytes leukops) lives only in the Fitzroy River system, Queensland, Australia — Wikipedia;
  Live Science (names it as the champion of cloacal respiration).
- It can stay underwater for days: Wikipedia ("up to three weeks"), Live Science (can "potentially remain underwater
  indefinitely"); Scientific American says the related white-throated snapping turtle can stay under "for days at a time".
  Script says only "for days".
- The cloacal bursae are the main site of underwater oxygen uptake in the white-throated snapping turtle (48 % of
  aquatic oxygen uptake) — Australian Zoologist / connectsci.au abstract (background only, not in the script).
- Dropped: exact percentages (70 % vs 100 % differ between sources) and "three weeks" (only one source).
