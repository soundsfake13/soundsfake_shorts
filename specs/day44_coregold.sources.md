# Day 44 — There's enough gold in Earth's core to coat the planet

Checked in this run (2026-11-15):

1. Discover Magazine, "Earth's Inner Fort Knox" (Anne Wootton, 1 Sep 2006):
   https://www.discovermagazine.com/planet-earth/earths-inner-fort-knox
   — geologist Bernard Wood (Macquarie University): "there's enough gold in Earth's core to coat its surface in
   1.5 feet of the stuff"; more than 99 percent of Earth's gold sank into the core.
2. ScienceAlert, "Earth's core holds a vast reservoir of gold, and it's leaking toward the surface" (2025):
   https://www.sciencealert.com/earths-core-holds-a-vast-reservoir-of-gold-and-its-leaking-toward-the-surface
   — "more than 99 percent" of Earth's gold is in its metallic core (citing Nature, doi 10.1038/nature04763);
   enough to cover the land about 50 cm (20 in) thick (citing a 2006 ABC report); the crust holds a tiny fraction.
3. IFLScience, "99 Percent Of Earth's Gold Is In Its Core":
   https://www.iflscience.com/99-percent-of-earths-gold-is-in-its-core-70361
   — quotes Prof. Bernard Wood to the ABC: "more than 99% of the Earth's gold is in the core"; ~50 cm layer estimate.

Wording choices: the plan said "Nature 2013" — the estimate actually traces to Wood's 2006 work (Nature 2006 /
ABC 2006 / Discover 2006), so no year is given on screen. Sources differ on "surface" (Discover, 1.5 ft ≈ 46 cm) vs
"land" (ScienceAlert/IFLS, 50 cm), so the video says "scientists estimate … the whole surface, about half a metre /
knee-deep" with "about" and "estimate". "Sank with the iron while Earth was molten" is stated as the reason gold went
to the core (Discover: gold "sank into the core" with iron early in Earth's history). Nothing else is claimed.
