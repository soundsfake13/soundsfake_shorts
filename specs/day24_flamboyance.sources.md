# Day 24 — A group of flamingos is called a flamboyance (sources checked 2026-11-20)

- Smithsonian's National Zoo & Conservation Biology Institute — "Why Are Flamingos Pink? And Other Flamingo Facts":
  https://nationalzoo.si.edu/animals/news/why-are-flamingos-pink-and-other-flamingo-facts
  (group = "flamboyance"; carotenoid pigments from algae and brine shrimp; chicks hatch with white-gray down and take
  several years to acquire their pink; feed with the head upside down; filter feeders)
- Smithsonian Magazine — "10 Things You Didn't Know About Flamingos" (2021):
  https://www.smithsonianmag.com/blogs/national-zoo-conservation-biology-institute/2021/07/13/10-things-you-didnt-know-about-flamingos/
  (same points: flamboyance, pigments, several years to turn pink, upside-down feeding)
- Encyclopaedia Britannica — "Why Are Flamingos Pink?": https://www.britannica.com/story/why-are-flamingos-pink
  (beta carotene from algae, larvae and brine shrimp; birds are born a dull gray)
- HowStuffWorks — "What Is a Group of Flamingos Called?":
  https://animals.howstuffworks.com/birds/what-is-a-group-of-flamingos-called.htm
  (flamboyance; carotenoid-rich brine shrimp and blue-green algae; chicks born gray; feed with heads upside down, filtering)
