# Day 25 — Koala fingerprints look almost exactly like ours (sources checked 2026-11-21)

- NOVA / PBS, "Koalas have fingerprints almost identical to ours" (2022): Henneberg found even careful microscope analysis could hardly tell koala ridges from human ones; explained as convergent evolution; wombats and kangaroos lack them. https://www.pbs.org/wgbh/nova/article/koala-fingerprints
- Live Science, "Koalas Have Human-like Fingerprints" (2011): Henneberg team; wombats/kangaroos lack prints; researchers' hypothesis: "biomechanical adaptation to grasping" branches and eucalyptus leaves. https://www.livescience.com/14007-koalas-human-fingerprints.html
- University of Adelaide repository — Henneberg, Lambert & Leigh, "Fingerprint homoplasy: koalas and humans" (Natural Science, 1997): prints "microscopically and macroscopically similar to those of humans", on fingers and toes. https://digital.library.adelaide.edu.au/dspace/handle/2440/5433
- Snopes fact check: no documented case of crime-scene examiners confusing koala and human prints (so the video does not claim one). https://www.snopes.com/fact-check/koala-fingerprints-crime-scene/
- San Diego Zoo Wildlife Alliance Library, Koala physical characteristics: first two fingers opposable to the other three; koala and human dermal ridges nearly identical even microscopically. https://ielc.libguides.com/sdzg/factsheets/koala/characteristics
- Queensland DETSI, "Down to Earth: koala facts": "Koalas have two opposable thumbs on each front paw". https://www.detsi.qld.gov.au/our-department/news-media/down-to-earth/koala-facts
