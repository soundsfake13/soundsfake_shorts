# Ep08 — Accidental inventions: sources checked (2026-11-26)

Every page below was fetched and read during this run. Where sources disagreed, the script softens or drops the detail.

1. **Bubble wrap began as failed textured wallpaper (1957)**
   - Smithsonian Magazine, "The Accidental Invention of Bubble Wrap" — https://www.smithsonianmag.com/innovation/accidental-invention-bubble-wrap-180971325/
   - Swiss Federal Institute of Intellectual Property (IGE), "From wallpaper to packaging: Bubble Wrap turns 70" — https://www.ige.ch/en/blog/blog-article/from-wallpaper-to-packaging-bubble-wrap-turns-70
   - Note: greenhouse insulation / IBM 1401 details are only in Smithsonian → left out.
2. **Sticky notes: a weak, reusable adhesive (1968) + falling hymnal bookmarks; nationwide launch 1980**
   - Lemelson-MIT, Art Fry & Spencer Silver — https://lemelson.mit.edu/resources/art-fry-spencer-silver
   - NPR, "An Idea That Stuck: How A Hymnal Bookmark Helped Inspire The Post-It Note" (2014) — https://www.npr.org/2014/07/26/335402996/an-idea-that-stuck-how-a-hymnal-bookmark-helped-inspire-the-post-it-note
   - Note: "1974" on the timeline is Fry's own date for his bookmark notes, quoted by NPR.
3. **Saccharin: chemist noticed sweetness on his food and hands; ~300× sweeter than sugar**
   - Britannica, Constantin Fahlberg — https://www.britannica.com/biography/Constantin-Fahlberg (1879; sweet taste on food and hands)
   - Mental Floss, "The Sweet and Not-So-Sweet History of Saccharin" — https://www.mentalfloss.com/food/sweet-and-not-so-sweet-history-saccharin (coal-tar products; ~300× sweeter)
   - Note: sources disagree on 1878 vs 1879 and on hand-washing → script says "late 1870s" and avoids the hand-washing claim.
4. **Super glue: rejected in 1942 (gun-sight plastic), rediscovered 1951 (jet canopies), sold 1958, reportedly used on wounds in Vietnam**
   - Lemelson-MIT, Harry Coover — https://lemelson.mit.edu/resources/harry-coover
   - Popular Science, "How a shot at making better gunsights became Super Glue instead" — https://www.popsci.com/technology/super-glue-invention-mistake/
   - Military Times, "How World War II led to the invention of Super Glue" — https://www.militarytimes.com/off-duty/military-culture/2021/07/30/how-world-war-ii-led-to-the-invention-of-super-glue/
5. **Microwave oven: melted candy bar near a radar magnetron; popcorn test; first oven ~6 ft tall, ~750 lb (~340 kg); inventor left school ~age 12**
   - APS News, "This Month in Physics History: first patent for the microwave" — https://www.aps.org/apsnews/2015/10/first-patent-for-microwave
   - IEEE Spectrum, "A Brief History of the Microwave Oven" — https://spectrum.ieee.org/a-history-of-the-microwave-oven
   - Lemelson-MIT, Percy Spencer — https://lemelson.mit.edu/resources/percy-spencer
   - Note: price ($3,000 vs $5,000) and the exploding-egg story disagree / single-sourced → left out.
6. **PTFE (non-stick coating): 1938 "empty" cylinder that still weighed full; cut open → white slippery powder; first used in the Manhattan Project**
   - APS News, "April 6, 1938: Discovery of Teflon" — https://www.aps.org/apsnews/2021/04/physics-history-teflon
   - Science History Institute, Roy J. Plunkett — https://sciencehistory.org/historical-profile/roy-j-plunkett
   - Smithsonian Magazine, "The Long, Strange History of Teflon" — https://www.smithsonianmag.com/science-nature/the-long-strange-history-of-teflon-the-indestructible-product-nothing-seems-to-stick-to-180984920/
7. **Implantable pacemaker: wrong resistor (1956) made the circuit pulse like a heartbeat; millions helped**
   - Lemelson-MIT, Wilson Greatbatch — https://lemelson.mit.edu/resources/wilson-greatbatch
   - University at Buffalo, "Internal Pacemaker" — https://www.buffalo.edu/research/about-us/units.host.html/content/shared/smbs/research_highlights/pacemaker.detail.html
   - Saturday Evening Post, "The Accidental Invention of the Lifesaving Pacemaker" (2019) — https://www.saturdayeveningpost.com/2019/09/the-accidental-invention-of-the-lifesaving-pacemaker/
8. **X-rays: Nov 1895 glowing screen; first image of his wife's hand with ring; news spread in weeks; shoe-store X-ray machines 1920s–1950s, later banned**
   - PBS NewsHour, "'I have seen my death': How the world discovered the X-ray" — https://www.pbs.org/newshour/health/i-have-seen-my-death-how-the-world-discovered-the-X-Ray
   - History.com, "German scientist discovers X-rays" — https://www.history.com/this-day-in-history/november-8/german-scientist-discovers-x-rays
   - IEEE Spectrum, "When X-Rays Were All the Rage, a Trip to the Shoe Store Was Dangerously Illuminating" — https://spectrum.ieee.org/tech-history/heroic-failures/when-xrays-were-all-the-rage-a-trip-to-the-shoe-store-was-dangerously-illuminating
9. **Penicillin: 1928 mould after a holiday; only enough for a single case in early 1942; a moldy Peoria market cantaloupe gave a far more productive strain**
   - PBS NewsHour, "The real story behind the world's first antibiotic" — https://www.pbs.org/newshour/health/the-real-story-behind-the-worlds-first-antibiotic
   - Live Science, Fleming and the petri dish (Sept 28, 1928) — https://www.livescience.com/health/science-history-alexander-fleming-wakes-up-to-funny-mold-in-his-petri-dish-and-accidentally-discovers-the-first-antibiotic-sept-28-1928
   - National Museum of Australia, Penicillin — https://www.nma.gov.au/defining-moments/resources/penicillin
   - USDA ARS, "The Enduring Mystery of 'Moldy Mary'" — https://scientificdiscoveries.ars.usda.gov/tellus/stories/articles/enduring-mystery-moldy-mary
   - USDA ARS timeline, penicillin — https://www.ars.usda.gov/oc/timeline/penicillin
   - Note: PBS gives "200 times" for the melon strain; others only say production "skyrocketed" → script says "far more".

   - Univ. of Toronto / CoolCosmos, Big Bang — https://dunlap.utoronto.ca/coolcosmos/bigbang.php ("a small fraction of that static")
   - Big Think, "Starts With A Bang: the Big Bang on an old TV" — https://bigthink.com/starts-with-a-bang/big-bang-old-tv/ (~1% on one channel)
