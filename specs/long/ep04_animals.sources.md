# Ep04 — 10 Animal Superpowers That Sound Fake (verified 2026-11-12)
1. Peregrine falcon: ~320 km/h (200 mph) terminal velocity in a stoop; 1999 experimental dive by falcon "Frightful" reportedly 389 km/h (242 mph), dropped from a plane — Guinness World Records https://guinnessworldrecords.com/world-records/70929-fastest-bird-diving ; Live Science (242 mph / 389 km/h; level flight 40–55 km/h) https://www.livescience.com/fastest-flying-animal . ("~4× a highway car at 100 km/h" = own arithmetic.)
2. Octopus: ~500 million neurons (≈ a dog), about two-thirds outside the brain in the arms/body; arms use reflex loops, learning happens centrally and is shared with arms — OIST https://oist.jp/node/35601 ; ~500 million, two-thirds in the arms, ~40 million per arm, "nine brains" — Univ. of Notre Dame https://sites.nd.edu/biomechanics-in-the-wild/2021/04/07/nine-brains-are-better-than-one-an-octopus-nervous-system/
3. Dung beetles + Milky Way (Dacke et al., Current Biology, Jan 2013): planetarium at Wits; Milky Way alone enough to stay on track; caps/visors → wandered; possibly first documented use of the Milky Way — National Geographic https://www.nationalgeographic.com/science/phenomena/2013/01/24/dung-beetles-watch-the-galaxy-thats-how-they-roll/ ; EarthSky https://earthsky.org/earth/dung-beetles-use-the-milky-way-to-navigate-at-night/ ; straight line to escape rivals who steal balls — Sci.News https://www.sci.news/biology/article00844.html
4. Electrophorus voltai (2019): up to 860 V, highest of any known animal; previous record 650 V (E. electricus); 7–8 ft long — ScienceDaily/Smithsonian https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2019/09/190910134323.htm ; Cornell CALS https://cals.cornell.edu/news/2019/09/scientists-shocked-discover-two-new-species-of-electric-eel . ("~7× a 120 V US outlet" = own arithmetic.)
5. Axolotl regrows limbs again and again, no visible trace; parts of brain, spinal cord, ovary, lung tissue — Smithsonian https://www.smithsonianmag.com/innovation/some-salamanders-can-regrow-lost-body-parts-could-humans-one-day-do-same-180974090/ ; leg, piece of heart, spinal cord, sections of brain, no scarring — MBL https://www.mbl.edu/news/axolotl-can-regrow-lost-leg-piece-its-heart-parts-its-spinal-cord-and-even-sections-its-brain-no-scarring-space-daily ; "exact missing limb" — National Geographic https://www.nationalgeographic.com/health/article/axolotl-regrow-limbs-retinoic-acid-human-medicine
6. Bombardier beetle: two chambers (hydroquinones + hydrogen peroxide / enzymes), ~100 °C spray, 368–735 pulses/s, valve snaps shut after each blast — National Geographic https://www.nationalgeographic.com/science/article/how-this-beetle-creates-500-explosions-per-second-in-its-bum ; "almost to the boiling point", Arndt et al. Science 2015 — ScienceDaily https://sciencedaily.com/releases/2015/04/150430145122.htm ; "up to 500 per second", "boiling-hot" — NBC News https://www.nbcnews.com/science/weird-science/x-rays-reveal-secrets-bombardier-beetles-machine-gun-bum-n351341
7. Mantis shrimp: club peak ~23 m/s (~80 km/h), 6,300–8,000 g, cavitation bubbles help break snail shells — ScienceDaily (Patek et al., Nature 2004) https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2004/04/040421233921.htm ; ~50 mph, up to ~10,000 g, collapsing bubbles give small flashes of light — National Geographic https://www.nationalgeographic.com/science/article/the-mantis-shrimp-has-the-worlds-fastest-punch ; bubble collapse releases heat, light and sound; each strike "essentially strikes twice" — American Scientist (S. Patek) https://www.americanscientist.org/node/3718
8. Wood frog: up to 60% of body freezes, heart stops, breathing stops, glucose up to 10×, thaws in spring — Audubon https://audubon.org/news/frogs-freeze-survive-alaskan-winter ; heart and brain stop, survives down to −16 °C, glucose replaces cell water — EarthDate (UT Austin) https://www.earthdate.org/episodes/frogsicles ; glucose acts like antifreeze — National Geographic https://www.nationalgeographic.com/animals/article/animals-freeze-then-thaw-explained
9. Tardigrades: 8 legs, ≤ ~1 mm, dry into "tuns" — Live Science https://www.livescience.com/57985-tardigrade-facts.html ; ESA FOTON-M3, Sept 2007, ~270 km, exposed to vacuum, cosmic & solar UV radiation; most survived vacuum + cosmic rays, some survived UV; reproduced afterwards; first animal tested in open space — ScienceDaily (Jönsson et al., Current Biology 2008) https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2008/09/080908135906.htm ; 10 days, eggs viable, "first animals to survive vacuum of space" — Discover https://www.discovermagazine.com/planet-earth/tardigrades-become-first-animals-to-survive-vacuum-of-space
10. Turritopsis dohrnii: ~4.5 mm; when injured/starving/stressed reverts to a polyp, which buds new genetically identical jellyfish — AMNH https://www.amnh.org/explore/news-blogs/immortal-jellyfish ; shrinks into a cyst/blob, new polyp in ~1.5 days, may never die of old age, still eaten by fish/turtles — Natural History Museum (London) https://nhm.ac.uk/discover/immortal-jellyfish-secret-to-cheating-death.html ; can repeat indefinitely, can still die from predation/disease — Britannica https://www.britannica.com/animal/immortal-jellyfish
