# Ep05 — 10 Moments in History That Sound Fake (verified 2026-11-16)

1. Anglo-Zanzibar War, 27 Aug 1896: British warships shelled the palace from ~9:00 (9:02), flag lowered / shelling stopped 9:40 → ~38 minutes, "shortest war in history"; Khalid bin Barghash had taken the throne without British approval and refused an ultimatum — History Today https://www.historytoday.com/archive/months-past/anglo-zanzibar-war ; Historic UK https://historic-uk.com/HistoryUK/HistoryofBritain/The-Shortest-War-in-History . (45 min = one half of football: own comparison.)
2. Emu War, Nov–Dec 1932, Western Australia: ~20,000 emus on wheat farms; three soldiers of the Royal Australian Artillery, two Lewis machine guns, 10,000 rounds supplied; ~30 birds after three days; campaign failed and was called off; emus regarded as the "victors"; Meredith quote "could face machine guns with the invulnerability of tanks" — Britannica https://www.britannica.com/topic/Emu-War ; National Geographic https://www.nationalgeographic.com/history/article/australian-emu-war-history
3. London Beer Flood, 17 Oct 1814 (Meux brewery, St Giles): vat burst, wave ~15 ft (~4.6 m), volume ~610,000 L (Atlas Obscura) to >320,000 gallons (Historic UK) → video says "more than half a million litres"; deaths 7–8+ → video says "several people" — Historic UK https://historic-uk.com/HistoryUK/HistoryofBritain/The-London-Beer-Flood-of-1814/ ; Atlas Obscura https://www.atlasobscura.com/places/site-london-beer-flood
4. Great Molasses Flood, Boston, 15 Jan 1919: ~2.3 million gallons (8.7 million L), wave at least 25 ft (7.6 m), first wave ~35 mph (~55 km/h), 21 killed, ~150 injured, molasses thickened in the cold air — AP via Boston 25 News https://www.boston25news.com/news/massachusetts/100-years-ago-in-boston-the-day-molasses-was-deadly-fast/904231962 ; South China Morning Post https://www.scmp.com/news/world/united-states-canada/article/2182032/great-molasses-flood-us-marks-100-years-deadly-wave ; Boston Magazine (35 mph, 2.3M gallons) https://bostonmagazine.com/?p=169781
5. Hero (Heron) of Alexandria's coin-operated holy-water dispenser, 1st century AD, described in Pneumatica: coin lands on a lever, weight tilts it, plug lifts, water flows until coin slides off — History (UK) https://www.history.co.uk/articles/ancient-rome-first-vending-machine ; Mental Floss https://www.mentalfloss.com/article/23449/first-vending-machine-and-other-surprising-firsts
6. Antikythera mechanism: ~2,000 years old, found 1901 by Greek sponge divers in a Roman-era shipwreck; 82 fragments (~1/3 survives), 30 bronze gears; showed Sun, Moon, planets, eclipses, Moon phases, Olympic dates; "world's first analogue computer" — UCL News (2021, Scientific Reports) https://www.ucl.ac.uk/news/2021/mar/experts-recreate-mechanical-cosmos-worlds-first-computer ; ScienceDaily https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2021/03/210312084716.htm
7. Fax before the telephone: Alexander Bain's British patent 1843 (applied 27 May 1843) for electric printing/"chemical telegraph" — the origin of fax — Britannica https://britannica.com/biography/Alexander-Bain-Scottish-inventor ; Today I Found Out https://www.todayifoundout.com/index.php/2014/11/shockingly-old-history-fax-machine/ (also "before the telephone and the American Civil War"); Gizmodo https://gizmodo.com/the-surpisingly-old-origins-of-the-fax-machine-1661053344 . Bell telephone patent 7 March 1876 — Mass Moments https://www.massmoments.org/moment-details/alexander-graham-bell-receives-first-patent.html ; IEEE History https://history.ieee.org/programs/ieee-global-museum/the-telephonic-bell/ . 1876 − 1843 = 33 (own arithmetic).
8. Carrington Event, 1 Sept 1859: Carrington saw the first observed solar flare; telegraph operators got shocks, sparks set paper on fire; Boston and Portland (Maine) operators disconnected batteries and worked on the storm-induced current; auroras as far south as the Caribbean — NOAA Research https://research.noaa.gov/?p=22740 ; Science History Institute https://sciencehistory.org/stories/magazine/that-time-demons-possessed-the-telegraph (two-hour conversation without batteries; aurora to Venezuela)
9. Mammoths & pyramids: last woolly mammoths on Wrangel Island until ~4,000 years ago (~2000–1650 BC), "over a thousand years after the building of the Pyramids at Giza" (EarthSky) — Stockholm University https://su.se/english/news/new-genetic-study-reveals-the-woolly-mammoths-journey-towards-extinction-1.746552 ; EarthSky https://earthsky.org/earth/woolly-mammoths-extinction-not-inbreeding ; Great Pyramid c. 2560 BC — Britannica https://www.britannica.com/print/article/234470 (checked for day43, 2026-11-14) & WorldAtlas (below). Video says "for centuries afterwards".
10. Cleopatra VII 69–30 BC — World History Encyclopedia https://www.worldhistory.org/Cleopatra_VII/ ; Great Pyramid ~2560 BC, Apollo 11 20 July 1969, Cleopatra ~2,500 yrs after the pyramid and ~2,000 yrs before the Moon landing — WorldAtlas https://www.worldatlas.com/articles/so-cleopatra-lived-closer-in-time-to-the-first-lunar-landing-than-the-great-pyramids.html . Arithmetic: 2560 − 30 = 2,530; 30 + 1969 − 1 = 1,998 (no year 0) → "about 2,000"; smartphones (2007): ~2,036 < 2,530 (own arithmetic).

Extra details used in the script (same sources as above):
- Zanzibar: ~2,800 (History Today) / ~3,000 (Historic UK) defenders → "around three thousand"; squadron of warships in the harbour (Historic UK lists HMS St George, Philomel, Racoon, Rush, Sparrow).
- Emu War: troops withdrawn 8 Nov, returned 12 Nov 1932 (Britannica).
- Hero: device spared priests from measuring each portion by hand (History UK).
- Antikythera: UCL team's new model of the front "Cosmos" display published 2021 (UCL News, ScienceDaily).
- Pantelegraph: Caselli demonstrated it for Napoleon III in 1860; tests Paris–Amiens and Paris–Marseille; approved for use across France (Today I Found Out; Gizmodo).
- Wrangel Island: rising seas isolated the island; founded by fewer than ten mammoths; survived ~6,000 years (Stockholm University; Science News Explores https://www.snexplores.org/article/why-woolly-mammoths-died-out , checked for day43).
- Double-decker bus ≈ 4.4 m tall: visual reference only (typical London bus height), not a claim.
