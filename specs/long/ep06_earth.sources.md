# Ep06 — 10 Facts About Earth That Sound Fake (verified 2026-11-19)

1. Speed: rotation at the equator ≈ 1,670 km/h (1,037 mph); orbit around the Sun ≈ 107,000 km/h (67,000 mph), ≈ 30 km/s — Space.com https://space.com/33527-how-fast-is-earth-moving.html ; BBC Sky at Night https://www.skyatnightmagazine.com/space-science/how-fast-does-earth-spin ("a little over 1,600 km/h"; "around 30 km/s… about 107,000 km/h"). Own arithmetic: 107,000 ÷ 3,600 ≈ 29.7 km/s; × 5 s ≈ 150 km (> marathon 42.2 km); × 24 h ≈ 2.57 million km.
2. Point Nemo: 2,688 km (1,670 mi) from the nearest land (Ducie Island, Motu Nui, Maher Island) → "about 2,700 km"; ISS ≈ 400 km up, astronauts can be the nearest humans; "spacecraft cemetery" — BBC Sky at Night https://www.skyatnightmagazine.com/earth-from-space/point-nemo ; Guinness World Records https://www.guinnessworldrecords.com/world-records/66641-remotest-spot-from-land ; Geography Realm https://www.geographyrealm.com/where-is-point-nemo/ ("Nemo in Latin means 'no one'"); Washington Post KidsPost (via kids.gwnews.com) https://kids.gwnews.com/articles/welcome-to-point-nemo (Latin "No One"; hundreds of pieces crashed there since the 1970s); CNN via KTVZ https://ktvz.com/news/national-world/cnn-world/2022/02/06/this-is-the-space-graveyard-where-the-international-space-station-will-be-buried/ (263+ pieces since 1971; ISS to end there); The Planetary Society https://www.planetary.org/articles/how-nasa-plans-to-deorbit-the-international-space-station (NASA will aim ISS debris at Point Nemo; disposal could start ~2030; timeline may change → video says "NASA plans").
3. Lightning: 44 ± 5 flashes per second worldwide — The Weather Network https://www.theweathernetwork.com/en/news/weather/forecasts/fast-facts-about-lightning ; ≈ 46 per second, ≈ 1.4 billion per year, nearly 4 million per day, from 17+ years of NASA TRMM Lightning Imaging Sensor data — University of Alabama in Huntsville https://www.uah.edu/news/research/earth-system-science-centers-lightning-research-is-vital . Video: "about 45 per second". Own arithmetic: 45 × 508 s ≈ 22,900 → "more than 20,000".
4. Plates move "roughly at the same rate that your fingernails grow" — USGS https://www.usgs.gov/faqs/how-fast-do-tectonic-plates-move ; Atlantic widens ≈ 4 cm/yr (Nature study, Univ. Southampton / Roma Tre) — IFLScience https://iflscience.com/environment/the-atlantic-ocean-is-getting-wider-as-europe-africa-and-the-americas-are-pushed-apart/ (other references give ≈ 2.5 cm/yr → video says "a few centimeters"). Own arithmetic: 2.5–4 cm × 534 years ≈ 13–21 m → "more than 10 m"; × ~80-year lifetime ≈ 2–3 m → "a couple of meters".
5. Green Sahara: African Humid Period ≈ 11,500–5,000 years ago; monsoon rains formed vast lakes; hippos, crocodiles, fish up to ~2 m; Wadi Sura "Cave of Swimmers" paintings ≈ 9,000–6,000 years ago; desert/green cycle roughly every 20,000 years — EarthDate (UT Austin) https://www.earthdate.org/episodes/swimming-in-the-sahara ; UC Irvine Earth System Science https://ess.uci.edu/news/858 (11,000–5,000 years ago; hippos, elephants; orbital cycle ≈ 23,000 years).
6. Day length 70 million years ago ≈ 23.5 h; 372 days per year; from daily growth layers in the fossil rudist shell Torreites sanchezi; Moon recedes 3.82 cm/yr; tidal friction slows Earth's spin — AGU via ScienceDaily https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2020/03/200309135410.htm ; Sci.News https://www.sci.news/paleontology/cretaceous-earth-day-08211.html
7. Mauna Kea ≈ 10,205 m base-to-peak, 4,205 m above sea level, ≈ 6,000 m underwater; Everest 8,848.8–8,849 m above sea level → difference ≈ 1.36 km — Guinness World Records https://guinnessworldrecords.com/world-records/tallest-mountain ; BBC Science Focus https://www.sciencefocus.com/planet-earth/what-is-the-tallest-mountain-in-the-world
8. Chimborazo (6,268 m, ~1° S) summit is the farthest point from Earth's center: ≈ 2,072 m (NOAA) / 2,163 m (LoC) farther than Everest → "about 2 km"; equatorial bulge from rotation; 8,849 − 6,268 = 2,581 m → "more than 2.5 km lower" — NOAA Ocean Service https://oceanservice.noaa.gov/facts/highestpoint.html ; Library of Congress Worlds Revealed https://blogs.loc.gov/maps/2020/11/extremities-of-the-earth-farthest-point-from-the-center-of-the-earth/
9. Inner-core boundary ≈ 6,000 °C (± 500), "as hot as the surface of the Sun"; laser-heated diamond anvil cell + X-rays at ESRF; melting measured at ~2.2 million atm, extrapolated to ~3.3 million atm; 2013, Science — Live Science https://www.livescience.com/29054-earth-core-hotter.html ; NBC News https://www.nbcnews.com/news/all/earths-core-1-800-degrees-hotter-thought-flna6C9615846 ; Gizmodo https://gizmodo.com/earth-s-inner-core-is-as-hot-as-the-surface-of-the-sun-482354178
10. Sinking molten iron took the vast majority of precious metals into the core; enough to cover Earth's surface in a 4-m-thick layer; accessible gold arrived later via a meteorite bombardment (>200 million years after Earth formed); evidence from tungsten isotopes in ~4-billion-year-old Greenland rocks (Willbold et al., Nature 2011) — University of Bristol https://www.bristol.ac.uk/news/2011/7885.html ; ScienceDaily https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2011/09/110907132044.htm . "More than twice as tall as an adult": own comparison (4 m vs ~1.75 m).
