# Ep03 — 10 Deep-Sea Facts That Sound Fake (verified 2026-11-09)
1. Better maps of Mars than the seafloor — AMNH curator John Sparks: "We have more detailed maps of the surface of Mars than we do the ocean floor" https://www.amnh.org/explore/news-blogs/seafloor-scanning ; USGS (light doesn't penetrate beyond the first few hundred feet; mapping relies on sonar) https://www.usgs.gov/news/science-snippet/why-we-have-better-maps-mars-seafloor-and-what-usgs-doing-change . 27.3% of the seafloor mapped to modern standards as of the June 2025 Seabed 2030 update — Nippon Foundation https://tnfsa.nippon-foundation.or.jp/news/2756701 ; Hydro International https://www.hydro-international.com/content/article/the-race-to-map-the-ocean-floor-tackling-the-remaining-70
2. Challenger Deep ~10,935 m (2021 pressure-sensor estimate; 10,994 m NOAA 2010 sonar), Everest ~8,848 m, >2 km difference; pressure >8 tons per square inch, >1,000× sea level — Live Science https://www.livescience.com/23387-mariana-trench.html ; EarthDate (UT Austin) https://earthdate.org/episodes/the-deepest-deep . ("~5 cars on a postage stamp" = own illustration: 8 tons ≈ 5 × ~1.5 t cars; a stamp ≈ 1 sq in.)
3. Diel vertical migration = the largest/greatest migration on Earth; billions of animals rise at night and return before sunrise; may create turbulence/move water — NOAA Ocean Exploration https://oceanexplorer.noaa.gov/facts/vertical-migration.html ; twilight zone 200–1,000 m, "the setting sun kicks off the largest migration on Earth" — MBARI https://www.mbari.org/?p=86958
4. 76% of water-column animals and 45% of seafloor animals observed by ROVs can make light — MBARI https://www.mbari.org/project/bioluminescence-and-fluorescence/ (Martini & Haddock 2017, Scientific Reports, srep45750)
5. Giant/colossal squid eyes up to ~27 cm, pupil ~9 cm, largest of any animal, ~3× swordfish (~9 cm); may spot sperm whales via disturbed bioluminescent plankton from ~120 m — Live Science https://livescience.com/19073-giant-squid-giant-eyes.html ; Scientific American https://www.scientificamerican.com/blog/observations/giant-eyes-help-colossal-squid-spot-glowing-whales/ (Nilsson et al. 2012, Current Biology)
6. Brine pools: "a small lake on the seafloor with a distinct surface and shoreline", mussel beds supported by methane — NOAA Ocean Explorer https://oceanexplorer.noaa.gov/explorations/02mexico/background/brinepool/brinepool.html ; Orca Basin brine pool 123 km² (~2× Manhattan), ~8× saltier than seawater, ~8,000 years old — Eos https://eos.org/articles/waves-of-deadly-brine-can-slosh-after-submarine-landslides ; "Jacuzzi of Despair" 4,200 ft deep, 4–5× saltier, toxic, crab carcasses on its floor — Christian Science Monitor https://csmonitor.com/Science/2016/1102/How-this-Jacuzzi-of-Despair-formed-beneath-the-Gulf-of-Mexico
7. Vent water heated by magma; >340 °C (NOAA Ocean Service) / >400 °C (NOAA Ocean Exploration fact sheet); doesn't boil because of pressure — https://oceanservice.noaa.gov/facts/vents.html ; https://oceanexplorer.noaa.gov/edu/materials/hydrothermal-vents-fact-sheet.pdf ; "approaching 400 °C", ambient ~2 °C — EARTH Magazine https://www.earthmagazine.org/article/benchmarks-february-17-1977-hydrothermal-vents-are-discovered . (Lead melts at ~327 °C — standard physical constant.)
8. 1977 Alvin dives, Galápagos Rift, ~2.5 km — EARTH Magazine (above); tube worms up to ~7 ft, "Garden of Eden", bacteria turn hydrogen sulfide into food — NPR https://www.npr.org/2011/12/05/142678239/the-deep-sea-find-that-changed-biology ; >2 m — NOAA fact sheet (above); no mouth or gut, symbiotic bacteria — AMNH OLogy https://www.amnh.org/explore/ology/ology-cards/186-giant-tubeworms
9. Whale falls last years to decades in the deep, ≥100 species, scavenger stages (sharks, hagfish, crabs) — Science News Explores https://www.snexplores.org/article/whales-get-a-second-life-as-deep-sea-buffets ; Southern California carcasses may last ~50–100 years — MBARI https://www.mbari.org/?p=86242 ; Osedax have no eyes, mouths or stomachs, root-like structures, bacteria; dwarf males inside females — MBARI/EurekAlert https://www.eurekalert.org/pub_releases/2004-07/mbar-wcy072604.php
10. Greenland shark: oldest in study ~392 ± 120 years; eye-lens radiocarbon incl. 1950s bomb pulse as marker; 28 sharks; longest-lived vertebrate known (vs bowhead whale 211); females mature ~156 — Science News https://www.sciencenews.org/article/study-ranks-greenland-shark-longest-lived-vertebrate ; ~1 cm/year growth, lens nucleus formed when a pup, published in Science Aug 2016 — PBS NOVA https://www.pbs.org/wgbh/nova/article/greenland-shark-is-officially-the-longest-living-vertebrate-on-earth . ("Some may be older than the United States": species lifespan ≥272 years vs 250 years since 1776 — own arithmetic, hedged with "may".)
