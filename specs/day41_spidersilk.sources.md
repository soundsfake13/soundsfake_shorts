# Day 41 — Spider silk vs steel: sources checked (2026-11-12)

Claims and where they were verified:

1. **By weight, spider silk is stronger than steel** (not by thickness — steel's tensile strength per area can be higher).
   - University of Bristol, School of Chemistry, Molecule of the Month "Spider silk": "stronger by weight than steel" — https://www.chm.bris.ac.uk/motm/spider/page2.htm
   - Smithsonian Magazine, "Brown Recluse Silk Is Stronger Than Steel…": "up to five times stronger than steel cable of similar weight" — https://www.smithsonianmag.com/smart-news/brown-recluse-silk-made-cable-180970871/
   - Forest Preserve District of Will County, "Myth buster: spider silk is as strong as steel": by area steel can win; "in terms of strength-to-density ratio, spider silk is far stronger than steel" — https://www.reconnectwithnature.org/news-events/the-buzz/myth-buster-spider-silk-is-as-strong-as-steel
2. **Some silk stretches up to ~4× its length without breaking** (capture silk).
   - Bristol (2–4 times), Will County myth-buster ("up to four times").
3. **A strand is far thinner than a human hair** — Will County (about 1,000× thinner), Smithsonian (recluse strands as small as 1/1000 of a hair). Script says only "much thinner than a human hair".
4. **Darwin's bark spider (Madagascar) makes the toughest known silk, >10× tougher than Kevlar.**
   - Agnarsson, Kuntner & Blackledge, PLoS ONE 2010, "Bioprospecting finds the toughest biological material" — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2939878
   - Live Science, "Itsy Bitsy Spider's Web 10 Times Stronger Than Kevlar" — https://www.livescience.com/8686-itsy-bitsy-spider-web-10-times-stronger-kevlar.html
   - Cosmos, "Madagascar spider silk 10 times stronger than Kevlar" — https://cosmosmagazine.com/biology/madagascar-spider-silk-10-times-stronger-than-kevlar
5. **It hangs its web over rivers on bridge lines up to 25 m long.** — PLoS ONE 2010 (anchor threads up to 25 m), Cosmos (bridge-lines up to 25 metres).

Wording kept careful: "pound for pound" / "for its weight" (never "strand for strand"); "about ten times tougher" (toughness = energy absorbed before breaking).
