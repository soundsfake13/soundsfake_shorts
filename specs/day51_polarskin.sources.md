# Day 51 (23:00) — Polar bears have black skin — sources checked this run (2026-11-22)

Claims and the sources that confirm them:

- **Polar bear skin is black** (nose and footpads show it): Britannica ("black skin that absorbs heat"), WWF ("a polar bear's skin is black, and its hairs are hollow"), SeaWorld ("A polar bear's skin, visible only on the nose and footpads, is black."), Guinness World Records.
- **The fur isn't white: each hair is pigment-free, transparent and hollow**: Polar Bears International ("Each hair shaft is pigment-free and transparent with a hollow core that scatters and reflects visible light."), Britannica ("Each hair shaft is hollow and transparent…"), SeaWorld ("Though really translucent, the hairs appear white…"), WWF (no white pigment, hollow hairs).
- **It looks white because the hairs scatter/reflect light, like ice**: Britannica ("…scatter and reflect sunlight, much like ice"), Polar Bears International.
- **On infrared / heat cameras polar bears are nearly invisible because the coat insulates so well**: Live Science (2014, "they still blended in with the surroundings"), Guinness World Records ("virtually invisible under infrared photography"), Mental Floss (cameras picked up heat from eyes, noses and breath only).
