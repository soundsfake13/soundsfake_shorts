# Day 53 — A fluffy cloud weighs about as much as 100 elephants (sources checked 2026-11-24)

- USGS Water Science School, "How much does a cloud weigh?" — https://www.usgs.gov/water-science-school/science/how-much-does-a-cloud-weigh
  A 1 km³ cumulus cloud at ~0.5 g of water per m³ (1 km³ = 1 billion m³) holds about 500,000 kg (1.1 million lb).
- Library of Congress, Everyday Mysteries, "How much does a cloud weigh?" — https://www.loc.gov/everyday-mysteries/meteorology-climatology/item/how-much-does-a-cloud-weigh/
  Same worked example (cloud ~1,000 m on each side, 0.5 g/m³ → ~500,000 kg); droplets and ice crystals are small enough to float on the slightest breeze.
- Smithsonian National Air and Space Museum, How Things Fly, "Ask an Explainer" — https://howthingsfly.si.edu/ask-an-explainer/how-much-does-cloud-actually-weigh
  Cumulus estimate 500,000 kg (1.1 million lb), "about the weight of 100 elephants".
- EarthDate (UT Austin Bureau of Economic Geology), "Puffy Clouds Weigh In" — https://www.earthdate.org/episodes/puffy-clouds-weigh-in
  ~500,000 kg ≈ 100 average-sized elephants; cloud droplets are so small that warm updrafts overcome gravity and keep them aloft.
- National Geographic, African elephant — https://www.nationalgeographic.com/animals/mammals/facts/african-elephant  (2.5–7 tons)
- Smithsonian's National Zoo, Asian elephant — https://nationalzoo.si.edu/animals/asian-elephant  (2,750–5,420 kg)
  → 500,000 kg / 100 = 5,000 kg per elephant, within the adult range.

Notes: the video says "about" for the half gram and 500,000 kg, and presents the 1 km cube as a "picture a cloud" example (it's the standard textbook estimate, not a measurement of every cloud).
