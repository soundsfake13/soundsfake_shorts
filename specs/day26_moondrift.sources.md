# Day 26 — The Moon is drifting away (sources checked 2026-11-22)

- NASA/JPL, "The Apollo Experiment That Keeps On Giving" (2019): Earth–Moon distance "increases by 1.5 inches (3.8 centimeters) a year"; reflector arrays left by Apollo 11, 14 and 15; lasers still fired at them from observatories; tidal bulges pull the Moon forward so it spirals away.
  https://www.jpl.nasa.gov/news/the-apollo-experiment-that-keeps-on-giving/
- NASA Goddard eclipse site, "Measuring the Moon's Distance" (LPI Bulletin 72): Moon "receding from Earth at a rate of about 3.8 centimeters per year"; beam is roughly four miles wide at the Moon; "only one photon is received every few seconds" even in good conditions.
  https://eclipse.gsfc.nasa.gov/help/ApolloLaser.html
- Live Science, "Will Earth ever lose its moon?" (2023): ~1.5 in (3.8 cm) per year, "roughly the rate at which fingernails grow, according to NASA"; lunar tides + tidal friction push the Moon outward.
  https://www.livescience.com/space/the-moon/will-earth-ever-lose-its-moon
