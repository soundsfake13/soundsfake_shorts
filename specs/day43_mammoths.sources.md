# Day 43 — Woolly mammoths were still alive when the Great Pyramid was built

Checked in this run (2026-11-14):

1. Stockholm University news, "New genetic study reveals the woolly mammoths' journey towards extinction" (2024, Cell study):
   https://su.se/english/news/new-genetic-study-reveals-the-woolly-mammoths-journey-towards-extinction-1.746552
   — Wrangel Island population went extinct "some 4,000 years ago"; fewer than ten mammoths founded it; it grew to
   ~300 reproducing individuals and survived ~6,000 years after the island was cut off by rising seas.
2. Science News Explores, "The last woolly mammoths offer new clues to why this species died out":
   https://www.snexplores.org/article/why-woolly-mammoths-died-out
   — last woolly mammoth died ~4,000 years ago on Wrangel Island, off Siberia; rising seas ~10,000 years ago
   stranded mammoths on islands; herd began with at most eight and grew to ~200–300.
3. Encyclopaedia Britannica, "Pyramids of Giza": https://www.britannica.com/print/article/234470
   — Giza pyramids built in the 4th dynasty, c. 2575–c. 2465 BCE; Great Pyramid built for Khufu.
4. Egyptian Museum (Rosicrucian), "Old Kingdom Monuments: Pyramids of Giza":
   https://egyptianmuseum.org/explore/old-kingdom-monuments-pyramids-of-giza
   — Giza pyramids built beginning ca. 2560 BCE; the Great Pyramid for Khufu took about twenty years.

Wording choices: "around 2560 BC" for the Great Pyramid; "about 4,000 years ago" for the last mammoths;
the gap is described as "centuries" (not a precise number), since both dates are approximate.
