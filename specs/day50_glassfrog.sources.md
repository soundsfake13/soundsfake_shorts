# Day 50 — Glass frogs have see-through skin — sources (checked 2026-11-21)

- Britannica, "Glass frog" — https://www.britannica.com/animal/glass-frog
  Family Centrolenidae, New World tropics; "the viscera are visible, and an observer can see the heart pumping blood";
  not all species have a translucent underside; most species 20–30 mm, a few nearly 80 mm.
- Duke University (Pratt School of Engineering), "Glassfrogs Hide Red Blood Cells in Their Liver to Become Transparent" —
  https://pratt.duke.edu/news/glassfrogs-hide-red-blood-cells-their-liver-become-transparent/
  Resting frogs store nearly 90 percent of circulating red blood cells in a "mirror-coated liver"; cells flow back out when active;
  photoacoustic imaging; published in Science, Dec 2022 (doi:10.1126/science.abl6620); species Hyalinobatrachium fleischmanni.
- American Museum of Natural History press release, "Glassfrogs Achieve Transparency By Packing Red Blood Cells Into Mirror-Coated Liver" —
  https://amnh.org/about/press-center/glassfrogs-transparency
  About 90 percent of red blood cells removed from circulation at rest; transparency increases two- to threefold; liver contains
  reflective guanine crystals; cells return to the blood when the frogs become active; glassfrogs live in the American tropics.
- NPR, "The astonishing vanishing act of the glassfrog, revealed" (Dec 26, 2022) — https://www.npr.org/2022/12/26/1145523973/glassfrog-hides-its-blood-glass-frogs
  Roughly 90% of red blood cells hidden; organs coated in highly reflective white crystals; heart visible pumping.

"tropical forests of the Americas" (sources: "American tropics", "New World tropics"); "most are 2–3 cm" (Britannica 20–30 mm);
